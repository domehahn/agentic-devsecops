The popular narrative that the internet has been “trained dry” and modern AI can therefore grow only through synthetic data is too simplistic. High-quality human data is neither completely exhausted nor fundamentally replaceable by synthetic data. What is true, however, is that for many forms of **reasoning training**, it is becoming increasingly attractive to generate tasks, solution attempts, preference signals, and verification data automatically. The main reason is not absolute data scarcity, but the economics of supervision.

A human expert can annotate only a limited number of mathematical proofs, code patches, or security analyses. A model, by contrast, can generate millions of candidates. If an environment can then automatically check which candidates are correct, a scalable learning loop emerges. This principle connects modern language models with an older tradition of **self-play and reinforcement learning**, made especially visible by AlphaGo and AlphaZero.[^1]

DeepSeek-R1-Zero demonstrated in 2025 how far this approach can go for language models. The model was trained with large-scale reinforcement learning without requiring conventional supervised fine-tuning as an initial stage, and developed pronounced reasoning patterns in the process. At the same time, the authors documented clear weaknesses, including poor readability and language mixing; DeepSeek-R1 therefore used a multi-stage process with cold-start data and additional training phases.[^2]

The relevant lesson is therefore not “humans are removed from training,” but: **automatically verifiable environments can amplify human supervision enormously.**

---

## 1. What Synthetic Data Actually Means

“Synthetic Data” is an umbrella term. In the context of LLMs, it can refer to at least five very different types of data:

1. **synthetic inputs** – for example, automatically generated mathematics problems,
2. **synthetic solutions** – answers or proofs generated by a model,
3. **synthetic preference pairs** – multiple answers with automatically generated rankings,
4. **synthetic reasoning traces** – intermediate steps or solution paths,
5. **synthetic environment interactions** – agents execute actions and receive machine-generated feedback.

These categories differ substantially in quality. A model that merely paraphrases its own text does not create new external truth. The situation is different when a generated hypothesis is subsequently checked by an **independent verifier**.

```text
Weak synthetic-data pattern:
LLM -> generates answer -> same LLM family declares it good

Stronger pattern:
LLM -> generates candidate -> compiler/solver/test/environment -> objective feedback
```

The verifier determines whether synthetic data merely reproduces model errors or actually enables a productive learning cycle.

---

## 2. Self-Play: Learning Without Prescribed Human Moves

Self-play was used successfully long before modern LLMs. AlphaZero learned Go, chess, and shogi by playing against itself, combined with reinforcement learning and a clearly defined winning condition.[^1]

The crucial point about board games is not that humans are absent, but that the environment provides **hard ground truth**:

```text
Game state -> action -> new state -> win / loss
```

The reward is unambiguous. This allows a system to discover strategies that do not need to be copied from human demonstrations.

Reasoning tasks are more complex, but some domains resemble this setup:

- mathematics with verifiable final answers,
- formal proofs with proof checkers,
- programming with tests,
- constraint-satisfaction problems,
- games and simulations,
- certain planning environments.

This gives rise to **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**: the quality of an output can be determined, at least in part, by external rules.

---

## 3. DeepSeek-R1-Zero: Why the Experiment Mattered

According to the authors, DeepSeek-R1-Zero was trained directly with large-scale RL on a base model, without SFT as a preparatory stage.[^2] During training, behavioral patterns emerged that can be interpreted as self-organized reasoning: longer solution paths, self-checking, and strategy changes.

This was scientifically interesting because it showed that complex reasoning traces do not have to be introduced exclusively through human-written chain-of-thought demonstrations.

But R1-Zero was not a finished product model. DeepSeek itself lists problems such as:

- low readability,
- mixing of multiple languages,
- weaker general user experience.

DeepSeek-R1 therefore added cold-start data, multiple RL/SFT phases, and distillation.[^2]

The architecture can be simplified as follows:

```text
Base Model
   ↓
RL with verifiable / rule-based rewards
   ↓
Reasoning behavior emerges
   ↓
Cold Start / SFT / additional RL stages
   ↓
more usable assistant behavior
```

R1 therefore does not refute the value of human data. It shows instead that **reasoning capability and linguistic product quality can be separate training objectives**.

---

## 4. Synthetic Tasks, Not Just Synthetic Answers

Synthetic data generation becomes especially valuable when systems generate not only answers but also new **task distributions**.

AlphaGeometry is a striking example. Google DeepMind generated millions of synthetic geometric theorems and proofs to address the scarcity of human-formalized geometry data. The resulting neuro-symbolic system combined a neural model with a symbolic deduction engine and solved 25 of 30 Olympiad geometry problems in the benchmark used.[^3]

Synthetic data therefore becomes a kind of curriculum generator:

```text
Generator -> new tasks
Solver    -> solution attempts
Verifier  -> valid / invalid
Trainer   -> learns from verified examples
```

A good curriculum can increase difficulty gradually and target regions of the problem space where the model remains weak.

---

## 5. Self-Evaluation and Self-Rewarding Models

Not every task has a deterministic checker. For open-ended dialogue, style, helpfulness, or strategic analysis, models are therefore also used as evaluators.

Yuan et al. investigated **Self-Rewarding Language Models** in 2024, where an LLM generates its own training signals through LLM-as-a-Judge prompting and is then iteratively optimized with DPO to improve both response quality and evaluation ability.[^4]

The concept is appealing, but it carries a fundamental risk: generator and judge may share the same blind spots. A closed system can optimize itself against internal heuristics without becoming objectively better.

“Self-rewarding” is therefore qualitatively different from a compiler or proof checker. The reward is **modeled**, not verified.

---

## 6. Model Collapse and the Quality Problem

As models are trained increasingly on synthetic data, a concern arises around so-called **model collapse**: errors can accumulate, and rare patterns can disappear from the distribution or be amplified over generations. Research on recursively generated data shows that naive retraining on model outputs can distort the original distribution.[^5]

That risk is not identical across all forms of synthetic data. Three factors make a major difference:

### Source Mixing

Human, real-world, and synthetic data are combined rather than fully replacing one another.

### Filtering and Verification

Only candidates with strong quality evidence enter training sets.

### Diversity

Multiple generators, sampling methods, and task sources reduce narrow self-copying.

Synthetic data is therefore not simply “good” or “bad”; it is a **data-governance problem**.

## 7. Synthetic Data for Coding Agents

Software engineering offers especially favorable conditions because many properties can be checked automatically.

A training or evaluation loop might look like this:

```text
1. Generate issue or bug scenario
2. Agent generates patch
3. Compiler checks types
4. Test suite checks behavior
5. Security scanner checks policies
6. successful / failed trajectory is stored
7. data flows into fine-tuning or evaluation set
```

It is important not to equate “tests are green” with “patch is correct.” A weak test suite can enable reward hacking. High-quality synthetic coding data therefore requires robust hidden tests, mutation testing, or additional static controls.

For DevSecOps, this creates an interesting connection: the same CI/CD infrastructure that validates human changes can act as an **automatic reward environment** for agents.

---

## 8. Self-Play for Agents: From Answers to Strategies

For autonomous agents, self-play can go beyond text generation. Two agents can assume different roles, for example:

```text
Attacker Agent <-> Defender Agent
Generator      <-> Reviewer
Implementer    <-> Test Author
Planner        <-> Adversarial Evaluator
```

This allows the system to generate new interaction data. Especially in security, however, caution is required: a red-team agent actively optimizes to discover weaknesses in its counterpart or environment. Such capabilities must be trained and evaluated in isolated sandboxes.

Again, the value depends on the quality of the **rules of the game**. A poorly defined reward signal produces optimized failure behavior rather than robust capability.

---

## 9. Synthetic Data Governance

Once synthetic data is used in production, it should be versioned and audited like any other training data.

A robust dataset manifest might contain:

```yaml
source_model: deep-reasoner-v3
generator_prompt_version: 12
verification:
  type: pytest-hidden-suite
  version: 7
accepted_samples: 184203
rejected_samples: 92114
human_audit_sample_rate: 0.01
created_at: 2026-08-22
```

Important questions include:

- Which model generated the data?
- Which version of the verifier was used?
- What is the verifier's error rate?
- Were personal or license-sensitive data introduced?
- Which samples were audited by humans?
- Can the dataset be reproduced?

Synthetic data reduces the need for manual annotation, but not the need for **provenance**.

---

## 10. Where Humans Remain Indispensable

Verifiable rewards work extremely well when objectives are clear. Many real-world values, however, are not formalized:

- Is an architecture maintainable over the long term?
- Is an explanation responsibly worded?
- Is a business decision appropriate?
- Does an apparently correct process violate organizational norms?

Humans therefore continue to define objective systems, inspect edge cases, and decide which reward functions are legitimate in the first place.

The trend does not necessarily lead to “training without humans,” but to a shift:

> Humans write fewer individual labels and more **rules, evaluators, benchmarks, and acceptance criteria** that can scale automatically.

In a sense, this is the same transformation seen in agentic engineering: from manually executing each individual step to designing the system that checks those steps reliably.

---

## Conclusion

Synthetic data and self-play are not a magical answer to allegedly “exhausted internet data.” Their real value lies in building learning loops in which models generate large numbers of their own candidates and use **independent verification mechanisms** as training signals.

AlphaZero demonstrated this principle in games; AlphaGeometry used synthetic theorems for geometry; DeepSeek-R1-Zero showed that large-scale RL can produce complex reasoning behavior without SFT as a mandatory first step.[^1][^2][^3]

The central limitation remains the quality of feedback. Where the world offers a hard checker, machine self-improvement can scale very far. Where the objective itself is subjective or incomplete, human governance remains essential.

## Notes on the Sources

The statement that human internet data is “nearly exhausted” cannot be expressed as a universally established fact. Research discusses data scarcity and quality limits, but the volume, usability, and legal accessibility of human data depend heavily on the model, language, licensing conditions, and data source. This article therefore treats synthetic data as a scaling and supervision strategy, not as an inevitable replacement for human data.
